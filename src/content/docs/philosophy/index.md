The technical sections here answer *how*. This one is for *why*, and for the
questions that don't resolve into a command you can copy.

There's a reason to keep both in one place. A runbook tells you what you did; a
principle tells you why you'd do it that way again. Split them across two systems
and the second kind quietly stops getting written, because it never feels urgent.

## What belongs here

- **Positions you've arrived at.** Something you've changed your mind about, and
  what changed it.
- **Ideas from elsewhere, in your own words.** A book or argument reduced to the
  part that stuck. If you can't restate it without the source, you don't have it
  yet.
- **Open questions.** Things you're still turning over. A page can be a question
  with three partial answers — that's more useful than nothing written down.
- **Principles you actually apply.** Especially ones that cost you something.

## What doesn't

Anything with a command in it. That goes in [How-To](../howto/ssh-keys.mdx) or
[Cheatsheets](../cheatsheets/git.md).

## On writing these

Unlike the technical pages, these aren't optimized for skimming. Write them as
prose, in full sentences, and let them be longer than feels efficient — the
value is partly in the act of writing, which is a different activity from
looking something up.

Date the thinking rather than pretending it's timeless. A page that says "as of
2026 I think X, and here's what would change my mind" ages into something
interesting. A page written as permanent truth just ages.

Revising an old page is not a correction. Add to it, or write the new position
alongside the old one and note what moved. The trail is the point.
